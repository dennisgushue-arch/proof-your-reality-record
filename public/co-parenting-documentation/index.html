How to Document Co-Parenting Incidents Clearly

Learn what to write down after a co-parenting incident, how to save messages and evidence, and how to build a clear chronological record.

What should I write down after a co-parenting incident?

  • Exact date and approximate time
  • Where it happened
  • Who was present
  • What was said or done, using neutral language
  • Relevant messages, screenshots, photos, or documents
  • What happened immediately afterward
  • Any follow-up communication or action

How detailed should a co-parenting incident log be?

  • Include enough detail that you could understand the event months later.
  • Avoid turning every entry into a long narrative.
  • Focus on specific facts, direct quotes when known, and supporting records.

Should I include screenshots and text messages?

  • Save messages that directly relate to the incident.
  • Keep the original message or screenshot when possible.
  • Connect each item of evidence to the incident it supports.

How do I keep the record neutral and factual?

  • Describe observable actions instead of assigning motives.
  • Separate what you personally observed from what someone else told you.
  • Label interpretation separately from factual details.

Why does a timeline matter?

  • Chronology helps distinguish isolated events from recurring patterns.
  • It makes follow-up events easier to connect.
  • Older incidents are less likely to be forgotten or confused.
