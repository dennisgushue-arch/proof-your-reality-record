How to Document a Landlord Dispute

Learn how to document landlord disputes, repair requests, notices, photos, messages, payments, and follow-up actions in a clear timeline.

What should I document in a landlord dispute?

  • When the problem started
  • What the issue was
  • When you contacted the landlord or property manager
  • What response you received
  • Photos or videos of relevant conditions
  • Receipts, notices, repair records, and messages

How should I document repair requests?

  • Record each request separately with its date.
  • Save written responses and appointment details.
  • Add what happened after each repair visit or missed appointment.

Should I keep photos and receipts?

  • Keep clear copies of relevant photos, invoices, receipts, notices, and communications.
  • Label them so you can tell which incident or request they relate to.

How do I document repeated follow-ups?

  • Log each call, email, message, or visit as a separate event.
  • Avoid replacing the history with one final summary.

Why build a timeline?

  • A timeline shows what happened first, what was reported, what was promised, and what happened next.
  • It is especially useful when a dispute lasts weeks or months.

Frequently asked questions

What records should I keep during a landlord dispute?

Keep dated repair requests, notices, messages, photos, receipts, and records of follow-up communication.

Should I document phone calls with a landlord?

You can record the date, time, participants, and a factual summary of the call.
